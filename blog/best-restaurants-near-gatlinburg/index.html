Best Restaurants Near Gatlinburg TN

Published July 14, 2025 • 4 min read

The dining scene near Gatlinburg has matured well beyond the expected tourist strip, offering everything from quietly serious Southern cooking to riverside dining rooms with mountain trout pulled from local waters. Whether you are spending a weekend or a full week in the Smokies, knowing where to eat makes a meaningful difference in how the trip unfolds.

Restaurants on the Parkway Worth Your Time

The Gatlinburg Parkway draws crowds, but a handful of restaurants along it hold up on merit rather than foot traffic alone. The Peddler Steakhouse has maintained a strong reputation for decades, serving hand-cut steaks in a candlelit setting above a rushing stream. The salad bar is a genuine throwback done well, and the atmosphere is unhurried in a way that feels rare in a tourist corridor.

Calhoun's on the River offers reliable Tennessee barbecue with a scenic view of the West Prong of the Little Pigeon River. The ribs are smoked low and slow, and the covered deck seating makes it a strong option on mild evenings. For something lighter, The Woodshed serves elevated comfort food — think cast iron cornbread and seasonal specials — in a space that leans more local than tourist.

Reservations are worth considering at most Parkway spots on weekends, particularly during peak summer and fall foliage season when wait times can stretch well beyond an hour.

Dining Just Outside Gatlinburg in Pigeon Forge and Sevierville

Some of the most compelling restaurants near Gatlinburg are a short drive away in Pigeon Forge and Sevierville. Old Mill Restaurant in Pigeon Forge centers its menu on stone-ground grains milled on site — the grits, cornbread, and porridge are worth the visit alone. It is a straightforward, ingredient-focused operation that has remained consistent for years.

In Sevierville, Applebarn Cider Bar and Winery offers a quieter, agricultural setting with local hard ciders, wines, and light bites. The surrounding orchard property gives it a sense of place that most area restaurants lack. It works particularly well as an afternoon stop rather than a full dinner destination.

Downtown Sevierville also holds a handful of independent restaurants less visited by tourists. The fare tends to be more casual but the crowds are thinner, and the locals who fill those rooms are generally a good endorsement in themselves.

Where to Eat for Breakfast and Casual Meals

Morning meals in Gatlinburg set the tone for a full day on the mountain. Pancake Pantry on the Parkway is an institution — lines form early on weekends and the menu spans well over two dozen pancake varieties. It is genuinely good, not just famous. Arriving before 8 a.m. on busy weekends tends to cut the wait considerably.

For a more relaxed breakfast, Myrt's Kitchen on East Parkway draws a local crowd with straightforward Southern breakfast plates at honest prices. The biscuits are made in-house and the service is the kind that remembers your coffee without being asked.

When a casual lunch is in order, Elvira's Cafe offers a small but well-considered menu of wraps, soups, and sandwiches in a low-key setting. It is the kind of place that rewards wandering off the main strip rather than staying tethered to the most visible options. Having a home base at elevation — with a full kitchen on hand — also means that not every meal has to be a reservation. Some evenings are best spent cooking in and watching the ridge go dark from the deck.
