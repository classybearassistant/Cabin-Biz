Best Things to Do Near Gatlinburg This Summer

Published May 25, 2026 • 5 min read

Summer in the Great Smoky Mountains arrives with long golden evenings, clear mountain air, and a landscape that rewards those who know where to look. The region surrounding Gatlinburg offers far more than a single town can contain — and the best experiences tend to unfold the moment you venture just beyond it.

Great Smoky Mountains National Park

The most visited national park in the country sits directly at Gatlinburg's edge, and summer is among the finest seasons to experience it. The park's 800 miles of maintained trails range from short woodland walks to serious ridge climbs, with Alum Cave Trail and the path to Laurel Falls drawing consistent attention for good reason. Wildflowers that began blooming in spring carry through June, and the canopy reaches full, dense green by July.

Clingmans Dome, the highest point in the park at 6,643 feet, offers sweeping views across multiple states on clear days. The drive along Newfound Gap Road alone is worth the time — it climbs through distinct ecological zones and reveals the scale of the range in a way that most visitors do not anticipate. Arrive early on weekdays to avoid peak congestion at the more popular trailheads.

Exploring Nearby Towns: Townsend and Bryson City

Among the best things to do near Gatlinburg is making the short drive to the quieter communities that border the park. Townsend, roughly 25 minutes west, sits at the park's "Peaceful Side" entrance and offers tubing on the Little River — a slow, sun-drenched float that has earned a loyal following among those who prefer their afternoons unhurried. The town is small and unpretentious, with good barbecue and minimal crowds.

Bryson City, just across the North Carolina state line, rewards those willing to make the 45-minute drive. The Nantahala Outdoor Center draws whitewater enthusiasts to the Nantahala River Gorge, where guided rafting trips run throughout the summer. The Great Smoky Mountains Railroad also departs from here, offering scenic excursions through mountain terrain that cannot be reached by car. Both towns operate at a pace that feels genuinely removed from Gatlinburg's busier corridors.

Scenic Drives and Overlooks Worth the Time

The Roaring Fork Motor Nature Trail, accessible from downtown Gatlinburg, winds through old-growth forest past historic homesteads and cascading streams. The one-way loop takes roughly an hour at a relaxed pace and delivers an authentic sense of the landscape that hiking alone cannot always provide. Pull-offs along the route offer quiet moments beside mountain creeks that feel genuinely secluded despite their proximity to town.

For those drawn to broader vistas, the Blue Ridge Parkway connects to the region's eastern edges and offers one of the most consistently scenic drives in the eastern United States. Cherokee, North Carolina — gateway to the Parkway's southern terminus — is approximately an hour from Gatlinburg and pairs well with a visit to the Museum of the Cherokee People, one of the most thoughtfully curated cultural institutions in the region. Summer evenings along these routes carry a particular quality of light that makes the journey its own destination.
