Best Waterfalls Near Gatlinburg

Published May 24, 2026 • 5 min read

Great Smoky Mountains National Park contains more than 100 named waterfalls, many of them within a short drive or moderate hike from Gatlinburg. Whether you are seeking a paved trail the whole family can manage or a more solitary backcountry experience, the options here reward the effort in ways that are difficult to overstate.

Laurel Falls — The Most Visited Waterfall in the Smokies

Laurel Falls is consistently cited as the most visited waterfall in Great Smoky Mountains National Park, and the reputation is well earned. The trail begins off Little River Road, roughly four miles from the Sugarlands Visitor Center, and follows a paved path for 1.3 miles each way. The elevation gain is gentle, making it manageable for most visitors regardless of fitness level.

The falls themselves drop 80 feet in two distinct tiers separated by a footbridge, with the upper section reaching slightly higher than the lower. The surrounding forest is old-growth, and the canopy provides meaningful shade even in summer. Arrive early in the morning on weekends — parking fills quickly, and the experience is considerably quieter before 9 a.m. No permits are required, though the park's standard entrance fee applies.

Grotto Falls — Walk Behind the Water

Grotto Falls stands apart from most waterfalls near Gatlinburg because the trail passes directly behind the cascade, through a shallow rock alcove worn smooth by centuries of water and foot traffic. The trailhead sits on Roaring Fork Motor Nature Trail, a one-way scenic road accessible from Historic Nature Trail Road in Gatlinburg.

The hike covers 1.3 miles one way through a dense hemlock forest, gaining roughly 500 feet in elevation. The terrain is unpaved and can be uneven, so appropriate footwear is advisable. The falls drop approximately 25 feet and maintain a strong flow through spring and early summer. Llama trains occasionally use this trail to supply the LeConte Lodge above — an unusual and memorable sight for those who happen to cross paths with them. The trailhead parking area is small, so a morning or weekday visit is recommended.

Ramsey Cascades — The Tallest in the Park

For those willing to commit to a more demanding outing, Ramsey Cascades offers the highest waterfall entirely within Great Smoky Mountains National Park at approximately 100 feet. The trail begins in the Greenbrier area, roughly six miles east of Gatlinburg via Highway 321, and covers four miles each way with over 2,000 feet of cumulative elevation gain.

The path moves through old-growth forest containing some of the largest trees in the eastern United States, including tulip poplars and yellow birches of notable scale. The final approach follows Ramsey Prong, crossing the stream multiple times on log bridges. The waterfall itself is a powerful multi-tiered cascade that surges in spring and after periods of heavy rain. This is a full half-day commitment and is best suited to experienced hikers with proper footwear, water, and weather awareness. The solitude and scale of the experience are well worth the preparation.
