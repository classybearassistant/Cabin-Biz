Things to Do in Gatlinburg with Kids

Published May 24, 2026 • 5 min read

Gatlinburg with kids is a different kind of mountain trip — one where the national park sits alongside hands-on museums, mountain coasters, and evenings that wind down with a view. The Smokies have a way of holding everyone's attention, regardless of age.

Great Smoky Mountains National Park

The most consequential thing you can do in Gatlinburg with kids costs nothing at all. Great Smoky Mountains National Park is the most visited national park in the country, and for good reason — its accessibility makes it genuinely welcoming to families with children of any age. Laurel Falls is a paved 2.6-mile round-trip trail with a payoff that requires no buildup: a double-tiered waterfall that impresses every time.

Clingmans Dome, the highest point in the Smokies at 6,643 feet, offers a short but steep half-mile walk to a viewing tower with 360-degree summit views above the clouds. For younger children, the Sugarlands Visitor Center provides junior ranger programs that turn the park into a structured discovery experience. Wildlife sightings — black bear, white-tailed deer, river otter — are common enough to feel like a reasonable expectation rather than a lucky accident.

Hands-On Attractions in Town

When the weather turns or the younger members of the group need a change of pace, Gatlinburg's indoor attractions hold up well. Ripley's Aquarium of the Smokies remains one of the most impressive regional aquariums in the Southeast, with a 340-foot moving walkway tunnel that passes through a 750,000-gallon shark habitat. It is the kind of place where children slow down on their own.

The Gatlinburg SkyLift Park offers a gondola ride followed by the SkyBridge — the longest pedestrian suspension bridge in North America at 680 feet — with views that are as unsettling as they are beautiful. For something more grounded, the Gatlinburg Space Needle observation deck gives families a quieter, elevated perspective on the town and surrounding ridgelines. Each of these attractions is within reasonable walking distance of the main strip, making it easy to move between them without significant planning overhead.

Mountain Coasters and Evening Wind-Down

Anakeesta, located just above downtown on a ridge accessible by chairlift or gondola, functions as a full-day destination on its own. The mountain village features a treetop skywalk, zipline, and a scenic rail ride through the canopy. The layout rewards unhurried exploration, and the elevation provides natural temperature relief during warmer months.

Gatlinburg has several mountain coaster options, including the Gatlinburg Mountain Coaster — a rider-controlled track that winds through forested terrain. Children meeting the height requirement tend to want immediate re-rides. For evenings, the Ole Smoky Moonshine Distillery on the main strip offers a family-accessible environment with live music and outdoor seating, while Gatlinburg's downtown provides enough variety — candy shops, fudge kitchens, and specialty food stops — to make a slow evening walk feel genuinely satisfying rather than obligatory. Back at the cabin, a private hot tub and a game room with arcade classics bring the day to a comfortable close.
