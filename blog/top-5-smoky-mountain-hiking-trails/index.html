Top 5 Smoky Mountain Hiking Trails Near Our Cabin

January 28, 2026 6 Min Read Trail Guide

The Great Smoky Mountains hold more miles of maintained trail than almost any other national park in the eastern United States — and the best of them begin within a short drive of Chalet Village. These five smoky mountain hiking trails offer a range of terrain, elevation, and solitude worth planning your stay around.

Alum Cave Trail to Mount LeConte

Few smoky mountain hiking trails carry the reputation of Alum Cave. The trailhead sits roughly 9 miles from Gatlinburg along Newfound Gap Road, making it one of the most accessible serious ascents in the park. The route climbs 2,763 feet over 4.4 miles to the summit of Mount LeConte — the third-highest peak in the Smokies at 6,593 feet.

The lower section moves through old-growth forest before arriving at the Alum Cave Bluffs, a curved overhang of black-streaked rock that feels genuinely dramatic. Above the bluffs, the trail narrows and steepens, with cable handholds assisting hikers along exposed ledges near the top. The summit rewards with sweeping ridgeline views that extend well beyond the park boundary on clear mornings. Plan for a full day and carry layers — temperatures at the top run significantly cooler than the valley.

Chimney Tops Trail

Chimney Tops is one of the more physically demanding short trails in the park. At 3.8 miles round trip with an elevation gain of roughly 1,400 feet, it moves quickly from a gentle creek-side walk into a sustained, rocky scramble toward twin rock spires that rise above the surrounding canopy. The final push to the top requires hands-and-feet climbing over open rock.

The views from the chimneys themselves are among the most exposed and striking in the entire park — ridge after ridge falling away in every direction, with Sugarland Mountain holding the western horizon. The trailhead is located off Newfound Gap Road, approximately 6 miles from the Gatlinburg park entrance. The trail saw significant wildfire damage in 2016, and the regenerating forest adds its own layer of visual interest to the landscape.

Ramsey Cascades, Rainbow Falls, Hen Wallow Falls, and Laurel Falls

Ramsey Cascades is the tallest waterfall in the park at 100 feet, reached by an 8-mile round trip trail through some of the most intact old-growth forest in the eastern United States. Massive tulip poplars and hemlocks crowd the upper trail, and the final half-mile involves boulder-hopping along the stream. The trailhead begins in the Greenbrier area, about 6 miles from Gatlinburg — an underused corner of the park that rewards those willing to seek it out.

Rainbow Falls is a 5.4-mile round trip trail originating directly from the Roaring Fork Motor Nature Trail, just minutes from the cabin. The 80-foot waterfall earns its name in morning light when mist catches the sun at the right angle. The trail gains roughly 1,600 feet and moves through rhododendron tunnels and hardwood canopy throughout.

Hen Wallow Falls offers a quieter 4.4-mile round trip in the Cosby area of the park, about 20 miles from Gatlinburg. The falls drop 90 feet into a wide plunge pool, and the trail remains far less crowded than the western corridors. For a shorter option close to the park entrance, Laurel Falls — a paved 2.6-mile round trip — delivers a genuinely beautiful 80-foot cascade accessible to most fitness levels. It is one of the most-visited trails in the Smokies, so early morning arrivals are advisable.
